Moving the controller to a Raspberry Pi 3

From Radio Team (Santiago, Andrian, Marcos)To Team C: electronics, protocols, PMVersion 0.1 · Sun 18 Oct 2026

The team wants a Raspberry Pi 3 in each box instead of the Pico 2. This page lists what that changes for each sub-team, with numbers from our link-budget model and the protocol simulator.

Short version. The radio side does not change: same E01-ML01SP4, same antenna, same margins, same NM spec. The work moves to power (an always-on 5 V rail and about 2 W instead of 0.3 W) and software (Linux timing, the SPI port, boot time, SD card). Cost per box roughly doubles but stays well under the 150 € cap. The USB stick gets simpler because the Pi has native USB host ports.

Working assumption. The Pi 3's WiFi/Bluetooth chip is switched off in software or desoldered, and the professors accept the board. The rule (p.6) reads: "The system can not include any chip implementing a standard wireless access protocol (e.g. WiFi, Bluetooth…)". Desoldering meets the wording; switching off in software depends on how the professors read it. Ask in writing on Atenea (draft below). Get the answer before the Quick Mode on 6 Nov, because the micro module shown there must be the final one (p.8): changing it later costs every bonus.

Which Pi 3

All Pi 3 boards share the same processor (BCM2837, 4 × Cortex-A53, 1 GB). They differ in wireless chip, current draw and size.

Suggestion: the 3 A+. It draws the least, is the smallest, and has no Ethernet or hub chip to power. If the team already owns 3 B boards, they work too: the BoM counts owned items at market price anyway (rules, "non-purchased items"), so owning them doesn't help the cost category. The 3 B+ draws the most; avoid it unless it's the only one we have.

The wireless chip

  • Software off (minimum, always do it): dtoverlay=disable-wifi and dtoverlay=disable-bt in config.txt, plus rfkill block all at boot. The chip stays on the board but stays silent. It would otherwise transmit in our own 2.4 GHz band.
  • Desoldering: hot-air job, on one spare board first. On the A+/B+ the chip is under a metal shield. After removal, check that the Pi still boots and that the SPI and USB work. We found no published guide, so treat it as an experiment.
  • Fallback if Atenea says "remove it" and desoldering fails: the Compute Module 3+ (same processor, no wireless at all, ~27–33 €, on a SODIMM carrier we would design). Stock in 2026 is patchy.

Power (electronics)

This is the biggest change. The Pico ran from the cell through VSYS and switched 5 V on only for the stick. The Pi needs a steady 5 V at all times, and Linux crashes or reboots when the rail sags. The Pi warns of undervoltage below 4.63 V (±5 %).

  SW --> BOOST["5.1 V boost, 2.5 A or more"]
  BOOST --> PI["Pi 3, 5 V on header pins 2 and 4"]
  SW --> LDO["3.3 V LDO, 300 mA or more"]
  LDO --> SP4["E01-ML01SP4"]
  SP4 --> ANT["Molex flex omni"]
  PI ---|"SPI0 + CE + IRQ"| SP4
  PI ---|"I2C"| FG["MAX17048 fuel gauge"]
  FG --- CELL
  PI ---|"native USB-A"| STICK["USB stick"]
  USBC["USB-C, charge only"] --> CHG["MCP73831 charger"]
  CHG --> CELL
  • 5 V boost: 5.1 V out, at least 2.5 A, from a 3.0–4.2 V input. Near the 3.0 V cutoff a 1 A load pulls about 2 A from the cell, so size the inductor and switch for that. A TPS61088-class part on our board, or a ready-made 5 V 3 A boost module for the bench. It replaces the TPS61023 + TPS2051 stick switch.
  • Feeding the Pi: through the 5 V header pins 2 and 4. That bypasses the Pi's own input protection, so put a TVS and reverse-polarity protection on our board. No cable to the micro-USB connector.
  • Radio supply stays as it is: its own 3.3 V LDO from the cell, 100 µF + 10 µF + 100 nF at the module. Never power the SP4 from the Pi's 3V3 pin (a few hundred mA shared with the board, and noisy).
  • Battery sense: a MAX17048 fuel gauge on I2C1 (GPIO 2/3) reads the cell voltage and state of charge (~2 €). The firmware thresholds (3.5 / 3.4 / 3.2 V) need re-measuring at T0 under the Pi's load, because the cell sags more at 0.5–1 A.
  • Charger: the MCP73831 at 500 mA can stay. It charges a 3000 mAh cell in about 6–7 h, which means overnight.
  • Gone: ideal diode to VSYS, PIO-USB pins, TPS61023, TPS2051, the USBLC6 on the stick port (the Pi has its own), SWD header, castellated footprint.

Software (protocols)

Our C code was written behind small interfaces (nrf24_hal_t, proto_env_t, usbm_disk_t), so most of it moves to Linux unchanged. It runs as one C program started by systemd at boot.

Unchanged

  • nRF24 driver logic: power cap, hop set, LBT/CCA, COT and idle accounting. All delays are minimums, so a late wake-up only makes it more conservative
  • Link ARQ + resume + POLL, NM Trickle gossip and relay log, NM spec frames
  • Codec: the system zlib, which the simulator already links
  • usb_msc rules: largest root .txt, .part + read-back CRC + rename, write-protect
  • phy_config.py, nrf24_config.h, all host tests

Port

  • HAL: add a buffer transfer so each nRF24 command is one spidev ioctl (byte by byte costs tens of µs per byte). CE and IRQ through libgpiod. delay_us busy-waits on CLOCK_MONOTONIC
  • Timers: proto_env_t on timerfd + epoll, random numbers from getrandom
  • Resume store: pwrite + fdatasync in a second thread (SD syncs take 10–100 ms)
  • USB: keep FatFs, reading the raw block device /dev/sda with no automount, so the 109 usb checks stay valid

Obsolete

  • proto/port/pico/ (TinyUSB + PIO-USB glue): removed 18 Oct, in git history
  • miniz and its low-memory mode
  • Flash and XIP rules: erase stalls, core 0/1 split, 2 KB stacks, DMA channel use
  • The "≥ 1 MB on-board storage" argument (the SD card holds gigabytes)

Timing on Linux

A stock kernel can pause our process for up to about 1.1 ms; with the real-time patch (PREEMPT_RT) the worst case is about 70 µs. These figures were measured on older Pi boards; we confirm on ours at T2.

Reboots: what a 10–30 s Linux boot costs

The rules ask the system to self-recover from interruptions (p.6). We added a reboot-time option to the simulator and a new test, T19. Times are for the whole 10 000-line file, sent compressed, with the receiver rebooting 15 s into the round.

ScenarioDown 0.3 s (Pico)Down 10 sDown 30 s
SRI, 5 % loss27.7 s—57.5 s
MRM as T14 (hopping, 5 % data / 30 % ACK loss, 10 % of hops busy)60.5 s78.0 s90.8 s

A reboot costs about its own downtime, and the resume logic picks up where it stopped. One reboot per round is survivable even at 30 s; two in MRM probably are not. Target a boot of 10 s or less (trimmed Pi OS Lite; Buildroot gets to 2–4 s if needed). Use the hardware watchdog through systemd (RuntimeWatchdogSec). Boot fits easily inside the 5-minute setup.

Linux setup checklist

  • Pi OS Lite (64-bit) or Buildroot. Kernel with PREEMPT_RT if the timers stay tight.
  • Read-only root with overlayfs (raspi-config nonint enable_overlayfs) and a small ext4 data partition for the resume state. A power cut or the drop test then cannot corrupt the card.
  • WiFi, Bluetooth and HDMI off (tvservice -o); no automount; our program owns the stick.
  • Before the status LED says "pull the stick": write, read back, sync.

Radio (radio team)

  • Link budget, margins, the conservative planning case (MRM +0.9 dB, NM −3.1 dB), the 20 dBm hopping profile and the NM spec are all unchanged. The micro was never part of the link.
  • New check at T0/T2: Pi noise in our band. Clock harmonics (6 × 400 MHz = 2400 MHz) and the HDMI clock could raise the noise on some hop channels. Nobody has measured it. Log RPD hits and PER on every hop channel with the Pi idle, under load, with the wireless chip disabled, and (if done) desoldered. Drop any bad channel from the hop set.
  • Keep the antenna high on the wall and the SP4 at the board edge, away from the SoC and the boost.
  • Radio hardware order is unaffected: 6 × E01-ML01SP4, 4 × Molex 146153-0100, 2 × U.FL-to-SMA pigtails. Drop the Pico 2s from the order.

Mechanics and the drop test

  • Fits the IKEA 365+ box (15 × 15 × 7 cm): the A+ is 65 × 56 mm, the B 85 × 56 mm; about 30 mm tall with our board on the header.
  • The Pi 3's microSD slot holds the card by friction only: tape or glue the card in place.
  • Board on M2.5 standoffs (58 × 49 mm hole pattern); our HAT on standoffs too, so it can't pull off the header.
  • U.FL plug glued and the cable tied (unchanged). Place the Pi so a USB-A port faces a wall cut-out for the stick during the foil-wrapped setup (Atenea Q6), or use a short internal extension to a panel socket.
