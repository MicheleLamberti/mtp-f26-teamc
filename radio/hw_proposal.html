v0.5 (18 Oct): the micro became a Raspberry Pi 3 (the team's request; the 3 A+ is suggested). The radio side did not change. On the board:

  • The carrier becomes a HAT-style board on the Pi's 40-pin header: SP4, radio LDO, 5 V boost, fuel gauge, charger, switches and LEDs.
  • A 5.1 V boost rated for at least 2.5 A now feeds the Pi all the time. It replaces the small boost and switch that only powered the stick.
  • The stick uses the Pi's own USB port, so PIO-USB, the stick switch and the USBLC6 are gone.
  • The Pi has no ADC, so a MAX17048 fuel gauge reads the cell instead of ADC3 and the ideal diode.
  • The Pi's WiFi/BT chip is disabled in software or desoldered. That is our working assumption; Atenea Q22 asks.
  • Cost goes to about 70–90 € per box. The full analysis is on the Team C Pi 3 Switch page.

v0.5.1 (18 Oct, later): the block diagram shows the HAT boundary, the microSD and the GPIO numbers; the pin map adds the mode switch's second GPIO (16), the fuel-gauge alert (25) and the charger status (26).

Earlier: v0.4.4 added the TX/RX role switch and the secured U.FL plug. v0.4.3 added low-battery countermeasures. v0.4.2 moved SRI/MRM to 20 dBm hopping on the same hardware. v0.4 chose one Ebyte E01-ML01SP4 and one flex omni per box.

The radio baseline is 2.4 GHz with the nRF24L01+. Under our split, the electronics team picks the micro and designs the board. This page is the radio team's suggestion, built around every hardware rule in the competition PDF, to save you time. It is not a decision. Please challenge it.

In short

  1. Micro module: Raspberry Pi 3, Model A+ suggested (Model B works if we already own them). It runs Linux on a microSD card and has native USB host ports. Its WiFi/BT chip is disabled in software or desoldered, which is our working assumption until Atenea Q22 is answered.
  2. Radio: one Ebyte E01-ML01SP4 per box, unchanged: a genuine nRF24L01+ with PA and LNA, SMD 14.5 × 18 mm, 8 castellations at 1.27 mm plus 2 GND half-holes, and an IPEX antenna socket. VCC is 2.0–3.6 V only, so it keeps its own 3.3 V regulator from the cell, never the Pi's 3V3 pin. The same module goes from the bench (on the breakout) to the final boxes.
  3. Board: a 2-layer HAT carrier on the Pi's 40-pin header, routed in KiCad, rev A, DRC clean, gerbers ready (below). It carries the SP4, the radio LDO, the 5.1 V boost that feeds the Pi, an ideal diode, the fuel gauge, the charger, ESD protection, and JST-XH leads to the panel controls. The antenna is the Ebyte TX2400-FPC-2509 flex, stuck on a box wall and plugged into the module, so there is no RF circuitry on our board.
  4. Power: one protected 18650 Li-ion cell (3000 mAh or more) on the box floor, charged on board through a charge-only USB-C port. The Pi and radio draw about 2 W, so one cell lasts about 4–5 h with the A+. For longer, wire a second holder in parallel into the same battery lead.
  5. Cost: ≈ €102 per device from the BoM (hw/BOM.csv), against the 150 € cap. That includes the Pi, the microSD, panel controls, mechanics and the lab PCB at €15.

The battery, the antenna and the Pi's own parts (microSD, USB port) sit outside the HAT. The software that runs on this hardware, teamc-node, is written and tested against an emulated radio; it has not run on a Pi yet (Team C Protocol Proposal v0.4.1).

Fab rulesfab, not lab0.2 mm clearance, 0.25 mm signals, 0.15 mm at the two 0.5 mm-pitch chips, plated vias. That fits a JLCPCB-class 2-layer fab (≈ €1/board, 1–2 weeks). At the lab-safe 0.3 mm this board does not route.
Gerbers for the carrierreadyhw/teamc-carrier/teamc-carrier_gerbers.zip (gerbers and drill) uploads as-is: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, HASL, 1 oz. In fab/: the placement file in JLCPCB's columns (DNP left out), the assembly PDF, and copper PDFs per layer.
Silkscreen and 3Ddone59 reference designators fit on the silkscreen at 1.0 or 0.8 mm. The other 26 have no room and are on the assembly drawing only. 3D models for every part, including the SP4 and the MAX17048 (our own).
Rule for everyone: with the HAT switched on, never plug the Pi's micro-USB. The A+ has no reverse blocking on that input, so our boost would push current into the supply. It is on the board's silkscreen too.

Battery sizing

Usable energy of one 3000 mAh cell is about 3.0 Ah × 3.6 V × 85 % boost efficiency ≈ 9.2 Wh. With the radio's ~0.15 W that gives:

  • Pi 3 A+, 1.7–2.2 W: 4.2–5.4 h
  • Pi 3 B, 2.0–2.4 W: 3.8–4.6 h
  • Pi 3 B+, 2.4–3.2 W: 2.9–3.8 h

A competition day of ~4 h powered on fits the A+ on one cell, barely. Use two cells in parallel, or switch the box off between rounds (boot ≤ 10 s). Measure the real draw at T0.

Low battery

The radio LDO from the cell is still fine: a clean 3.3 V down to a cell of about 3.4 V, about 2.95 V at the 3.0 V cutoff, inside the SP4's 2.0–3.6 V. The new weak point is the 5 V boost: if it sags below 4.63 V the Pi throttles, and lower it crashes.

  • Battery sense: MAX17048 on I2C1 (no ideal diode needed any more). The firmware also reads the Pi's undervoltage flag (vcgencmd get_throttled) and logs it.
  • Firmware: thresholds (3.5 / 3.4 / 3.2 V) re-measured at T0 under the Pi's load, since the cell sags more at 0.5–1 A. Below the lowest, fsync received data after every burst.
  • T0 measures the radio output and the Pi's current at 3.6, 3.3 and 3.0 V.
  • Boost: TPS61022 with a 1 µH / 11.5 A inductor (peak ≈ 5.7 A at 3.0 V in, 2.5 A out); 100 µF + 100 nF at the Pi's 5 V pins. The cell path (fuse + two Si2333DDS) drops ≈ 0.15 V at 2 A.
  • No hardware under-voltage cut-off: the cell's protection board and the software thresholds do it, so switch the box off after a round.
  • Radio fallback: the board has a TPS63001 buck-boost footprint (DNP). Fit it instead of the LDO's 0 Ω link if T0 shows more than 2 dB less output at 3.0 V.

For the sustainability report

The Pi 3 box uses about 7× the energy of the Pico design (≈ 2 W against ≈ 0.3 W). In its favour: the Pi is reflashable, repairable and reusable after the course, and the HAT unplugs. We can measure watts per round directly with the fuel gauge.
