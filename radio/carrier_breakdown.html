Power

Everything runs from one Li-ion cell, 3.0–4.2 V. The Pi needs a steady 5 V and draws 0.3–1 A; the radio needs its own clean 3.3 V and pulls 120 mA bursts when it transmits.

Radio and Pi interface

The SP4 sits bottom-right, its IPEX socket at the board corner and at least 45 mm from the Pi's wireless chip. No track runs under the module on either layer (Ebyte's layout rule); ground pour and vias do. SPI has 33 Ω series resistors; CSN and IRQ have 10 kΩ pull-ups to the Pi's own 3.3 V, so an unpowered Pi is not fed through them.

The board

65 × 56 mm in the official HAT outline with M2.5 holes on 58 × 49 mm, 2 layers, 1.6 mm. 696 track segments (1.67 m), 146 vias, ground pours on both layers. It is drawn for a JLCPCB-class fab: at the lab-safe 0.3 mm rules this board does not route, and its two 0.5 mm-pitch chips need 0.15 mm and plated vias anyway.

Board minimums: 0.2 mm track, 0.8 / 0.4 mm vias, 0.5 mm to the edge, 0.8 mm text. JLCPCB allows 0.1 / 0.1 mm.

Where things sit

  • Top-left, by header pins 2/4: the boost in TI's layout (inductor above the IC, input cap at VIN, output caps under VOUT) and the ideal diode.
  • Left edge: battery lead J20 → fuse → Q1 → Q2, then the charger and the USB-C below them.
  • Middle: fuel gauge U6, radio LDO U4, the buck-boost option U5 (not fitted), test points TP1–TP3.
  • Bottom edge: panel connectors J22 (power), J10 (button), J11 (role), J12 (mode).
  • Bottom-right: the SP4 with its IPEX at the corner; under the header: the pull-ups and the UART header J13.

The SP4 breakout (bench board)

Single-sided, 31 × 37.5 mm, lab-safe 0.3 / 0.4 mm rules, DRC 0. A 1 × 8 header in the usual nRF24 pin order plus a 1 × 2 bench-supply header, with 10 µF + 100 nF + 100 µF at the supply. It is ready to etch in the lab (gerbers and 1:1 copper PDFs in hw/sp4-breakout/fab/) and carries the radio for the T0 and T2 bench tests before the carrier exists.

Decisions made finishing the routing (21 Oct)

On 19 Oct the power path and SPI were routed and about 30 small nets were left for you. Finishing them changed the layout in the places below. The schematic, the parts, the outline and the rule values did not change.

DecisionWhyEffect for you
Net classes rewrittenA /power/* pattern had put every net of the power sheet in the 1 mm class: FET gates, the charge LED, PROG, the charger status. The fine-pitch class was 0.4 mm wide. This, not board space, is why no router could finish.Classes as in the table above, in build_carrier.py.
An autorouter for the small netsFreerouting routed every net except power and ground. The power copper stayed fixed; ground is left to the pours. Its clearances were padded by 10–20 µm, because it rounds a few µm under the rule, and its 0.19 mm neck-downs at pads were widened back to 0.2 mm.Correct but not pretty: some lines detour (about 60 mm on the mode, role and charger-status lines). Tidy with push-and-shove if you like.
SPI and radio lines autorouted at 0.25 mmOf about 10 routing variants, keeping only the power copper fixed left the fewest open connections.7–64 mm long, fine for SPI at a few MHz; the 33 Ω series resistors are in place.
Fuel gauge ground pins tied to its exposed padU6 pins 1, 4 and 6 are GND. Short 0.2 mm tracks under the package join them to the centre pad, which has a via. Pin 6's outward stub went, which freed the way out for FG_ALRT (pin 5).Standard practice; FG_ALRT now leaves east to a via.
Buck-boost option rearranged (all not fitted)L2 turned 90° and moved 0.5 mm, as in TI's layout, so each pad faces its pin. C15 turned 180°. U5's GND pins tied to its centre pad. One 3V3_RADIO via moved onto R16's pad edge (R16 is a 0 Ω link, not fitted). R15's VBAT_SW feed became a 0.25 mm track instead of a 1 mm branch, which is enough for a 100 Ω filter.Only matters if T0 says to fit the buck-boost.
Two FET gates routed by handThe ideal-diode gate (ZVD_G) and the power-switch gate (PWR_G) sit inside the wide power copper, so the autorouter could not reach them; the project's own grid router did.None.
Ground joined explicitlySignal tracks cut the pours into pieces. 71 GND vias plus 57 short GND tracks (76 mm in total) join every piece to the main ground. KiCad's own connectivity checked it, not an estimate.Two places hang on these thin links: review point 1 below.

Before you order

  1. Ground links. The radio LDO's ground (U4 pin 2, C11, C12) and the area under the middle of the header (J1 pins 20/25, C50–C52, J13) reach the main ground through short GND tracks and vias. Thicken them or add vias if you see a better path.
  2. Charger heat. U1 (MCP73831) only has the pour around it; add copper if you want the full 452 mA instead of its thermal fold-back.
  3. Track tidy-up (optional). Shorten the autorouter's detours; run DRC after every change.
  4. Box. Measure the IKEA box inside and fix the panel-connector positions (J10–J12, J22) before freezing the outline.
  5. One SP4 measured (14.5 mm, IPEX position ±0.5 mm) before the order.

The schematic is generated from Python: edit hw/teamc-carrier/sheets/*.py, then run the build. The board scripts run in this order:

python3 hw/scripts/build_carrier.py        # schematic, ERC, board footprints
python3 hw/scripts/place_carrier.py        # placement, pours
python3 hw/scripts/route_carrier.py        # power path
python3 hw/scripts/autoroute_carrier.py    # Freerouting (needs Java 25 + the jar)
python3 hw/scripts/finish_carrier.py       # last pins, ground, clean-up
python3 hw/scripts/silk_carrier.py
python3 hw/scripts/fab_carrier.py
python3 hw/scripts/render_board.py hw/teamc-carrier/teamc-carrier.kicad_pcb --with-pi

The autorouter is not deterministic: a rerun gives a different, equivalent board, and the hand fixes are tied to today's placement. Check DRC after every rerun, or edit the board in the KiCad GUI instead.
