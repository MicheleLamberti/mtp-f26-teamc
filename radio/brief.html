Both are built and tested. See 20 dBm. Also new: an NM link-protocol diagram.

New in v0.8 (16 Oct): every team uses 20 dBm, so every team hops. Atenea Q3 confirmed 20 dBm in writing, and the other teams will use it too. For a narrowband radio that means hopping, so the fixed MRM channel plan is gone: our hop set now covers all 40 even channels (2402–2480 MHz), the teams share the band by listen-before-talk, and the boxes need ≥ 10 m between teams. New in v0.4: all four teams agreed to run every mode at 2.4 GHz, so 868 MHz is dropped. Bottom line

  1. 2.4 GHz is the right band for this competition. In MRM all teams transmit at once rule. At 868 MHz four teams would share one 250 kHz channel (about a quarter of 67 kbps each). At 2.4 GHz the four teams share 83.5 MHz by hopping, there is no duty-cycle limit, and NM has one common radio for everybody.
  2. Margin, now with 20 dBm. SRI and MRM hop at up to 20 dBm e.i.r.p. (CNAF UN-85 a), approved by a professor). With the SP4 and one omni at 0.75 m: SRI 70 m +36.5 dB, MRM 260 m +13.9 dB (+8.4 dB at today's RF_PWR −12 until T0 measures the module). NM stays at 10 mW on one channel (UN-85 b): +9.9 dB.
  3. MRM is no longer the thin link. +13.9 dB at 0.75 m, +6.9 dB even at 0.5 m. All teams transmit at the same 20 dBm, so the near-far ratios are back to the equal-power ones: keep ≥ 10 m between teams' boxes at each end (never 2 m).
  4. Speed is no longer a problem. 250 kbps gives ~107 kbps of goodput (~96 kbps hopping). The simulator delivers the whole file in 27 s at 5 % loss, and all 10,000 lines even without compression (71 s).
  5. One omni, decided. Plugged straight into the SP4 (no adapter) and with the SP4's −96 dBm sensitivity, it is only 1 dB behind the two-patch build on MRM, with none of the patches' NM risks. Remaining risk: if T0 measures only ~+2 dBm out of the module, every link loses ~5 dB.
  6. MRM channel plan: none any more. Every team hops at 20 dBm with listen-before-talk; ours uses 37 channels in its own order. Hops that land within 4 MHz of another team's collide: about 13 % of hop pairs at 10–20 m, which costs up to ~31 % of our ACKs at the sender end (pessimistic). The ARQ absorbs it, with a short POLL for lost ACKs: all lines in 58 s in the simulator (115 s with 30 % loss both ways).
  7. Plan for less. With the datasheet sensitivity 3 dB worse, the antenna's worst direction (0 dBi) at both ends and a 6 dB body or fade loss, MRM at 20 dBm keeps +0.9 dB and NM falls to −3.1 dB. Antenna height, people kept away from the boxes, and tests T4/T5 decide (link budget).

Working baseline decided

The E01-ML01SP4 is a genuine Nordic nRF24L01+ with a PA and an LNA, as a 14.85 × 18 mm SMD module with an IPEX (U.FL) antenna socket. Datasheet: +20 dBm max, −96 dBm at 250 kbps, 120 mA TX, 26 mA RX, 2.0–3.6 V verified. Its lowest power setting gives about +2 to +7 dBm, depending on the PA gain; test T0 measures it before any field test assumption. With the 3 dBi peak of the omni that is at most 9.5 dBm e.i.r.p. That is the 10 mW setting for NM, Quick Mode and the fallback. The hopping profile for SRI and MRM may go up to 20 dBm e.i.r.p. (below); the driver refuses anything higher on every other profile.

Four radio profiles are generated by radio/phy_config.py. Each is checked against the band edges (Carson bandwidth plus ±60 ppm crystal drift per box) and the 10 dBm e.i.r.p. limit for its antenna path.

No duty-cycle limit

Neither UN-85 b) nor adaptive hopping under UN-85 a) has a duty-cycle condition, so the 3-rounds-per-hour limit of 868 MHz is gone. Test as often as we like. The firmware's duty-cycle guard was removed.

The power limit is the only rule to watch. On the 10 mW profiles the driver refuses any setting above RF_PWR −18 dBm; only the hopping profile goes higher, and only after a clear-channel check on each hop.

Firmware ready before the hardware

A portable C driver for the nRF24L01+ passes 244 host checks against a simulated radio, including two radios talking over a simulated channel, the power limit, and the listen-before-talk rules of the hopping profile. It needs five board functions (SPI select and transfer, CE pin, microsecond delay and clock).

Frames use dynamic length and the radio's CRC, and go out without the chip's own ACKs, so our ARQ stays in charge. The protocol simulator runs on it: all 20 scenarios pass, five of them hopping.

Antenna

One Molex 146153-0100 flex dipole per box (34.9 × 9 mm, 2.4–3.0 dBi peak, > 66 % efficiency), plugged into the SP4's socket, stuck vertically as high as possible on a side wall. The same antenna serves SRI, MRM and NM, and Quick Mode too.

The model uses 2 dBi in the box assumption; test T5 measures it.

Why not the E01-2G4M27D

Its −99 dBm receiver is tempting, but its lowest output is about +9 dBm. Behind a ~3 dBi antenna that is ~12 dBm e.i.r.p., over the 10 mW limit. It would be legal in the 20 dBm hopping mode, but it could not run NM, Quick Mode or the fallback at 10 mW, so it stays out.

20 dBm with frequency hopping baseline · 16 Oct

CNAF UN-85 has two regimes in 2400–2483.5 MHz verified. b) generic low-power devices: under 10 mW e.i.r.p.: NM, Quick Mode and the fallback. a) wideband data systems under EN 300 328: 100 mW e.i.r.p., but the density is capped at 100 mW per 100 kHz for frequency hopping and 10 mW per MHz for anything else. Our signal is under 1 MHz wide, so without hopping a) still means ~10 dBm. The 20 dBm needs a hopping radio that follows the EN 300 328 sharing rules.

Without hopping, EN 300 328 also allows 20 dBm in total, but caps the density at 10 dBm per MHz (cl. 4.3.2.3), as does the EU decision that UN-85 a) points to. Our signal is about 0.6 MHz wide, so on one fixed channel it is 5–10 dB over (option B below). EN 300 328 V2.2.2 has two kinds of hopping. Non-adaptive hopping caps power × duty: at 20 dBm the radio may transmit only 10 % of the time (~16 kbps), too slow for a 2-minute file. Adaptive hopping with listen-before-talk has no duty limit, so that is what SRI and MRM use (D-R6). A professor approved 20 dBm e.i.r.p. on 16 Oct.

What changes

  • Nothing in the hardware. The SP4 is a +20 dBm module that we hold at its lowest step; hopping only raises the step. TX bursts move toward the datasheet's 120 mA, which the radio LDO already covers.
  • Driver: only the hopping profile may go above −18 dBm. It refuses channels outside the hop set and any frame without a clear check on the current hop, ends each occupancy at 50 ms and waits the idle gap. 244 host checks pass.
  • Protocol: unchanged; the radio layer hops every 30 ms (one 16-frame burst). The simulator gives each box its own drifting slot clock; the receiver parks on one channel and re-syncs. All 10,000 lines in 38 s with 10 % of hops busy, 69 s with 25 % busy plus a receiver reboot. Still to build on the Pico: the slot clock and hop acquisition (only simulated so far).
  • NM stays on one channel (77) at 10 mW. Hopping in sync across four teams' boxes for a 0.5 kB file is not worth it, and NM has +9.9 dB.

Why it is allowed, and what to keep

  • Regulation: the design meets the EN 300 328 adaptive-FHSS rules in the table, at ≤ 20 dBm e.i.r.p. measured with our antenna.
  • Course: a professor approved 20 dBm e.i.r.p. on 16 Oct, and the answer to Atenea Q3 confirmed it in writing (the rules say oral comments do not override the written rules). Cite Q3 in the ANNEX.
  • Evidence: T13 (e.i.r.p.), T17 (where the clear-channel check trips) and T18 (hop pattern, dwell, skipping a busy channel on the analyser), plotted for the ANNEX.
  • Other teams: they use 20 dBm too, so they hop with listen-before-talk as well. Agree ≥ 10 m between boxes at each MRM end (see the MRM section).

Rules that constrain the radio

  • Unlicensed spectrum only, compliant with CNAF 2017 UN notes, IEEE C95.1 (RF exposure) and ESD safety. rule
  • No chip implementing a standard wireless access protocol (WiFi, Bluetooth, 2G–5G, ZigBee; the Session 1 slide adds NB-IoT and LoRa). The nRF24L01+ has none of them. rule
  • Nothing may stick out of the 15 × 15 × 7 cm IKEA 365+ box except buttons, switches and connectors, so the antennas go inside. The box sits on a support of about 70 cm. rule
  • Modules shown at Quick Mode are final. Antennas, batteries and mechanics may still change. rule
  • Distances: SRI 70 m, teams one after another, 2 minutes; MRM ~260 m, all teams at the same time, 2 minutes; NM relays along the South and East rims with no line of sight end to end, 5 minutes. rule
  • Scoring counts only the first unbroken run of byte-identical lines (~10,000 lines of ~100 B), so reliability beats raw bit rate. rule

Pessimistic: it ignores the clear-channel check, which skips a hop when another team is on the same channel nearby. At 30 m the collisions fall to 8 % of hop pairs (20 % of ACKs). At any spacing, ~7 % of our ACKs meet another sender on the very same channel, which the clear-channel check cannot see while we listen. Hence the POLL: after a lost ACK the sender asks for it again with one 2-byte frame instead of resending 16. Simulator T14 (5 % data loss, 30 % ACK loss, 10 % busy hops): all 10,000 lines in 58 s; with 30 % loss both ways, 115 s. Agree ≥ 10 m between teams' boxes at each end (30 m between the senders if the organisers allow it), a different hop order per team, and listen-before-talk for everyone. Test T15 checks it with a third box hopping 10 m away.

Antenna height is the biggest lever

At 0.75 m the ground reflection takes over beyond ~18 m at 2.4 GHz. Past that point loss grows 40 dB per decade and hardly depends on frequency: PR ≈ EIRP·GR·(h₁h₂)²/d⁴.

Raising both antennas from 0.75 to 1.0 m adds +5 dB on MRM. Mount the omni as high on the wall as the box allows. Whether "about 70 cm" allows a taller support is Atenea Q4.

The nRF24 has no RSSI

It only has one bit (RPD) that says "above −64 dBm at the chip", about −76 dBm at the SP4's antenna socket (LNA gain to be measured). Range tests therefore measure packet loss, and levels come from the spectrum analyser or the model. RPD still works as listen-before-talk and for the campus channel survey.

Datasheet limits that matter

  • 32 B maximum payload: our frames shrank from 64 B to 32 B (28 B of data). verified
  • Variable-length frames need the chip's auto-ACK feature on, so the driver sends every frame as "no ACK": no chip ACKs or retries on air.
  • PA always in line: no bypass, so the lowest output is +2 to +7 dBm; the driver caps the setting there.

Throughput: does the file fit in 2 minutes?

About 1 MB in 120 s needs 66.7 kbps of useful throughput. The model assumes selective-repeat ARQ with one ACK per 16 frames, 28 B of file per 32 B frame, a 0.3 ms gap between frames, and 85 % efficiency for the micro and USB assumption. The simulator runs the real protocol code over a modelled channel with 5 % random loss, deflate compression, and the nRF24 airtime from the driver.

Handoff to electronics

One E01-ML01SP4 per box. SMD, 10 pads at 1.27 mm: 1 VCC, 2 CE, 3 CSN, 4 SCK, 5 MOSI, 6 MISO, 7 IRQ, 8–10 GND (Ebyte manual §3). No traces under the module on the top layer.

PinAt the microWhat matters
VCC, GNDsupply2.0–3.6 V only, never the battery rail (a Li-ion cell reaches 4.2 V). Use a dedicated 3.3 V LDO for the radio (≥ 300 mA, dropout ≤ 0.1 V at 150 mA) straight from the cell: it regulates down to ~3.4 V and then passes the cell through (≥ 2.95 V at cutoff, fine for the SP4). Battery sense on ADC3 with an ideal diode into VSYS; firmware warns below 3.5 V, and 100 µF + 10 µF + 100 nF at the module. 120 mA in TX, 26 mA in RX.

NM link protocol (gossip, no routing)

sequenceDiagram
  participant O as Origin box (SW corner)
  participant S as Next box (South rim)
  participant C as Boxes further on (corner, East rim)
  participant R as Receiver (NE end)
  Note over O: Setup: NM-file from the USB stick into flash, cut into ~20 chunks of 25 B
  Note over O,R: GO. Every box on RF_CH 77, 250 kbps, 10 mW, no chip ACKs, no addresses
  loop every Trickle interval I (1 s, doubling to 8 s)
    O-)S: NM_DATA x 20 [0x4E, file_id, idx, total, 25 B, CRC-16]
  end
  Note over S: new chunk: check, store, reset I to 1 s
  S-)O: rebroadcasts what it holds (O goes quiet after 3 copies per chunk)
  S-)C: the same frames reach one box further
  C-)R: relayed along the rim
  Note over R: all chunks and the whole-file id check out: NM-file to flash
  Note over O,R: STOP: every box writes the NM-file and NM_LOG.TXT to a USB stick

Every box ends up with an identical copy, which is how a team proves it took part (rules: "delivering a copy of the NM-file from at least one of their node devices"). The relay log is extra evidence. Details: the protocol proposal page.

Atenea forum (posted by the project leader)

  • Allowed chips. Is the nRF24L01+ on the Ebyte E01-ML01SP4 allowed? Its packet format is Nordic-proprietary and it has no Bluetooth.
